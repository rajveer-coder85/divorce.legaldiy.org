A Joint Petition is not simply about both spouses signing a divorce document.

Both of you should understand the arrangements you are asking the court to recognise.

Use this guide to identify what you have already agreed and what still needs discussion.

This is an educational readiness guide.

It does not determine whether a court will grant your divorce.

## 1. The Decision to Divorce

### CHECKLIST
* We both want to proceed with the divorce.

* We both understand that this is a Joint Petition.

* We are both willing to participate in the process.

* Neither of us is being forced or pressured into the agreement.

### RESULT
If you cannot agree on the decision to proceed together, the Joint Petition pathway may not be suitable.

## 2. Children

### DISPLAY ONLY IF RELEVANT
If you have children, ask:

* We understand where the children will primarily live.

* We have discussed how they will spend time with the other parent.

* We have discussed weekends, holidays and important occasions where relevant.

* We understand how important decisions concerning the children will be handled.

* We have discussed their financial needs.

* We both understand the arrangements being proposed.

### IF NOT COMPLETE
Don't rush this decision.

## 5. Property

### CHECKLIST
* We have identified our major assets.

* We know what should happen to the matrimonial home.

* We understand any outstanding mortgage.

* We have considered other significant property and financial assets.

* We understand relevant debts.

* We both understand the proposed division or arrangement.

### IF NOT COMPLETE
[Understand Property & Matrimonial Assets &rarr;](/joint-petition/what-we-need-to-agree#property)
## 6. Is the Agreement Genuine?

### CHECKLIST
* We have had an opportunity to understand the arrangements.

* Neither spouse is deliberately hiding important financial information.

* Neither spouse is being threatened or forced to agree.

* Both of us understand that the arrangements may create continuing obligations after divorce.

### WARNING
If you feel unable to make decisions freely, or there is serious disagreement, coercion, hidden assets or another issue preventing genuine agreement, do not treat Joint Petition as simply a faster way to complete the divorce.
