There is an important difference between:

"We both want a divorce."

and:

"We both understand and agree on how the divorce should work."

For a couple considering a Joint Petition, the second question matters.

## Agreement Should Be Informed

You should understand the decisions you are making.

That includes arrangements concerning:

* children;
* maintenance;
* property;
* financial responsibilities;
* and continuing obligations.

## Agreement Should Be Specific

"We'll work it out later" may leave important matters unresolved.

Where an issue matters after divorce, think about what the arrangement actually means in practice.

## Agreement Should Be Practical

An arrangement should be capable of working in real life.

For example:

"Access whenever we want"

may sound cooperative today.

But ask:

What happens when work schedules change?

What happens during school holidays?

What happens if the parents disagree later?

The objective is not to create unnecessary rules.

It is to make sure both spouses understand the arrangement they are making.

## Agreement Should Be Voluntary

Neither spouse should agree merely because they feel they have no choice.

Where there is serious pressure, threats, coercion or an inability to make decisions freely, independent professional assistance may be appropriate.
