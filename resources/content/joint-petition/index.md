If you and your spouse have both decided that the marriage should end, you may be able to approach the divorce together.

But agreeing to divorce is only the beginning.

Before proceeding with a Joint Petition, both of you should understand what needs to be decided about your children, maintenance, property and the arrangements that will continue after the marriage ends.

LegalDIY helps you understand these decisions before you move forward.

## Do Both of You Want the Divorce?

A Joint Petition begins with both spouses being willing to proceed with the divorce together.

Ask yourselves:

* Have we both decided that the marriage should end?
* Are we both willing to proceed together?
* Are we willing to discuss the terms of the divorce?
* Are we both able to make these decisions voluntarily?

If the answer is yes, the next question is whether you can agree on the important arrangements arising from the divorce.

## You May Agree to Divorce — But Have You Agreed on What Happens Next?

Two people can agree that they want a divorce while still disagreeing about important issues.

For a Joint Petition, you should work through the arrangements that affect your family and finances.

There are four main areas to think about.

### CHILDREN

If you have children, consider:

* Where will they live?
* How will they spend time with each parent?
* How will important decisions concerning them be handled?
* What access arrangements will work?
* How will their financial needs be met?

[Understand Children Arrangements &rarr;](/joint-petition/what-we-need-to-agree#children)
### MAINTENANCE

Consider:

* What are the children's financial needs?
* Who will contribute to those needs?
* How will school and medical expenses be handled?
* Is spousal maintenance relevant to your circumstances?

[Understand Maintenance &rarr;](/joint-petition/what-we-need-to-agree#financial-support)
### PROPERTY

Consider:

* What happens to the matrimonial home?
* What other significant assets exist?
* Are there outstanding housing or other relevant loans?
* Will property be retained, transferred or sold?
* Do both spouses understand the proposed arrangement?

[Understand Property &rarr;](/joint-petition/what-we-need-to-agree#property)
### THE AGREEMENT

Before proceeding, ask:

* Do we both understand what we are agreeing to?
* Are the arrangements practical?
* Is either spouse still uncertain about an important issue?
* Is either spouse agreeing because of pressure rather than genuine agreement?

## Joint Petition Does Not Mean You Must Agree About Everything in Life

Divorce often follows a difficult period.

You do not need to agree about why the marriage failed.

You do not need to resolve every disagreement you have ever had.

The important question for a Joint Petition is whether you can reach genuine agreement on the matters that need to be dealt with for the divorce.

The objective is not to repair the relationship.

It is to determine whether both of you can make clear, informed and workable arrangements for ending the marriage.
