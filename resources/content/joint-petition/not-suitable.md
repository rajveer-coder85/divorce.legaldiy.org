LegalDIY currently focuses on couples who are able to proceed together.

Not every divorce fits that pathway.

Recognising this early can save you time.

## One Spouse Does Not Agree to the Divorce

If one spouse is unwilling to proceed jointly, you cannot simply turn the matter into a Joint Petition by completing documents for both spouses.

A different divorce process may need to be considered.

## You Cannot Agree About the Children

A serious dispute concerning matters such as:

* where the children should live;
* access;
* parenting arrangements;
* or important aspects of their welfare

may mean that you are not currently ready to proceed together.

[Understand Children Arrangements &rarr;](/joint-petition/what-we-need-to-agree#children)
## You Cannot Agree About Money

If there is a significant unresolved dispute concerning child maintenance or spousal maintenance, understand the issue before attempting to proceed jointly.

[Understand Maintenance &rarr;](/joint-petition/what-we-need-to-agree#financial-support)
## You Cannot Agree About Property

If both spouses claim the same property or substantially disagree about how assets should be divided, this may require resolution before a genuine joint arrangement can be presented.

[Understand Property &rarr;](/joint-petition/what-we-need-to-agree#property)
## Important Financial Information Is Being Hidden

Agreement requires both people to understand what they are agreeing about.

Where substantial assets are concealed or disputed, a straightforward self-help Joint Petition pathway may not be appropriate.

## One Spouse Is Being Forced to Agree

A Joint Petition should represent genuine agreement.

If a spouse is signing because of threats, coercion or serious pressure, this should not be treated as an ordinary agreed divorce.

## This Does Not Mean You Cannot Get Divorced

It means that LegalDIY's current Joint Petition pathway may not fit your circumstances.

A single-petition or contested process exists for situations where spouses cannot proceed together.

LegalDIY provides general information about that distinction, but our current self-help divorce pathway focuses on agreed Joint Petition matters.
