Maintenance should not begin with:

> “What is the normal amount?”

Start instead with:

> “What financial needs exist, and what arrangement are we proposing?”

## Child Maintenance

Parents should consider their children's actual financial needs and the contribution each parent proposes to make. The arrangement should be understood rather than based on an assumed universal figure.

## What Expenses Should We Think About?

Depending on the family, relevant needs may include:

- food and housing;
- clothing;
- education;
- transport;
- medical needs;
- childcare;
- activities; and
- other reasonable recurring needs.

Malaysian civil-divorce law does not provide one standard amount that fits every child and every family.

## Regular and Irregular Expenses

Separate monthly or regular support from additional or irregular expenses, such as particular medical or education costs.

Both spouses should understand who will meet these expenses and how decisions will be communicated.

## Spousal Maintenance

Spousal maintenance means financial support between spouses or former spouses where applicable. It may or may not be relevant, and it is not automatic in every divorce.

Both spouses should understand whether maintenance is proposed and what that arrangement means.

## Readiness Questions

- We understand the children's ordinary financial needs.
- We have discussed how those expenses will be met.
- We have considered significant additional expenses.
- We understand whether spousal maintenance is relevant.
- Both spouses understand the proposed arrangement.
