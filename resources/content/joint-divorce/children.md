If you have children, divorce changes the relationship between the spouses—not the children's relationship with their parents.

Before proceeding together, understand the arrangements you are proposing for your children. This guide helps cooperative parents make informed decisions; it does not teach custody litigation.

## Start With the Children's Daily Life

Ask:

- Where will the children primarily live?
- What will their normal weekly routine look like?
- How will they spend time with each parent?
- How will school routines work?
- How will transport work?

## Understand the Legal Concepts

### Custody

Custody generally concerns parental authority and responsibility for important decisions about a child.

### Care and Control

Care and control generally concerns the child's day-to-day care and the parent with whom the child ordinarily lives.

### Access

Access concerns the child's time and communication with a parent who does not have day-to-day care.

These concepts should be understood separately, even where parents are cooperating.

## Access Arrangements

Parents may need to consider ordinary weekends, relevant weekdays, school and public holidays, birthdays, important celebrations, communication and travel.

There is no universal schedule for every family. Arrangements should reflect the circumstances and welfare of the children.

## Important Decisions

Discuss education, medical care, important welfare decisions and how parents will communicate about them.

## Readiness Questions

- We understand where our children will live.
- We understand how they will spend time with each parent.
- We understand the access arrangement.
- We have considered important decisions concerning them.
- We have considered their financial needs.
- Both of us understand the arrangement.
