A Joint Petition is the route used when both spouses approach the divorce together and jointly ask the court to dissolve their marriage.

The important idea is:

> You are proceeding together.

It should not be understood merely as a “cheaper divorce” or “faster divorce.” The educational emphasis is cooperation and agreement.

## Joint Petition Means Both Spouses Participate

Both spouses approach the court jointly. One spouse is not bringing the divorce against the other.

Both should understand the petition, participate voluntarily and understand the arrangements they are asking the court to recognise.

## Agreement to Divorce Is Only the Starting Point

You may both agree that the marriage should end but still need to work through important questions, including:

- children;
- child maintenance;
- spousal maintenance where relevant;
- matrimonial property;
- debts; and
- continuing responsibilities.

## What Joint Petition Does Not Mean

### “Everything is automatically 50/50.”

No. Property arrangements need to be understood in light of the assets, liabilities, contributions and circumstances involved.

### “We do not need to think about children because we agree.”

No. Cooperative parents still need to understand the arrangements proposed for their children.

### “We can leave every issue to work out later.”

Important continuing arrangements should be understood before proceeding.

### “We need to agree about who caused the marriage to fail.”

Not necessarily. The focus of this journey is whether both spouses can proceed together and agree on the arrangements required.
