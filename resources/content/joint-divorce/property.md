Before deciding how property should be dealt with, first understand what property and liabilities actually exist.

Do not begin with:

> “Who gets half?”

Begin with:

> “What do we have, what do we owe, and what arrangement are we proposing?”

## Identify the Major Assets

Consider the matrimonial home, other real property, savings, investments, vehicles, significant business interests, relevant retirement-related assets and other substantial assets.

## Identify the Liabilities

Consider housing loans, vehicle financing, relevant debts and other financial obligations.

## The Matrimonial Home

Ask:

- Who owns it?
- Is there a mortgage?
- Will it be sold?
- Will one spouse keep it?
- Can the proposed arrangement actually be implemented?
- Does ownership need to change?
- Are financing arrangements involved?

An agreement between spouses does not itself require a lender to release a borrower or automatically change registered ownership.

## Contributions

Malaysian matrimonial-asset law may consider financial and non-financial contributions. It should not be reduced to the statement that everything is automatically divided exactly in half.

## Property Owned Before Marriage

Property acquired before marriage is not automatically treated in exactly the same way as property acquired during marriage. Under section 76 of the Law Reform (Marriage and Divorce) Act 1976, substantial improvement during the marriage by the other spouse or by joint efforts can be relevant.

## Readiness Questions

- We have identified our major assets.
- We understand the matrimonial home.
- We understand significant outstanding debts.
- We have discussed what should happen to the major assets.
- Both spouses understand the proposed arrangement.
- We are not deliberately concealing important financial information.
