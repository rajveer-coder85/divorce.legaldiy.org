There is a difference between:

> “We both want a divorce.”

and:

> “We both understand and agree on what happens next.”

Before moving toward a Joint Petition, check whether your agreement is clear, informed, practical and voluntary.

## Is It Clear?

Both spouses should understand what has been decided. If their understanding is materially different, they may not yet have reached the same agreement.

## Is It Specific Enough?

Avoid relying entirely on “we will work it out later.”

Not every small detail needs a rule, but important continuing arrangements should be understood.

## Is It Practical?

One spouse keeping the matrimonial home may sound straightforward. But is there a mortgage? Is refinancing required? Does ownership need to be transferred? Can the arrangement actually be implemented?

Think beyond the sentence and consider how the arrangement would work in practice.

## Is It Voluntary?

Neither spouse should be forced, threatened or seriously pressured into an agreement. Both must be able to make decisions freely.

## Are You Both Reading the Same Agreement?

Each spouse should be able to explain, in their own words:

- what happens to the children;
- what financial support is proposed;
- what happens to the home;
- what happens to significant assets; and
- what continuing responsibilities remain.
