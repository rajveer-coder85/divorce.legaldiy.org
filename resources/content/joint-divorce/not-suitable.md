LegalDIY currently focuses on couples who are able to approach divorce together.

Not every divorce fits this pathway. Recognising that early can save you time.

## Your Spouse Does Not Want to Proceed Together

A Joint Petition requires both spouses to approach the divorce jointly. One spouse cannot create genuine joint participation by completing or signing for the other.

## Serious Disagreement About Children

If parents substantially disagree about where children should live, access, important decisions or welfare, they may not currently have the agreement needed for this pathway.

## Serious Disagreement About Maintenance

An unresolved dispute about child or spousal maintenance should be understood and addressed before treating the matter as an agreed divorce.

## Serious Disagreement About Property

Where both spouses make incompatible claims to the same property or cannot agree on significant assets and liabilities, a straightforward Joint Petition pathway may not be appropriate.

## Important Assets Are Being Hidden

Genuine agreement requires both spouses to understand the significant financial picture. Concealed, transferred or substantially disputed assets can require independent professional assistance.

## One Spouse Is Being Forced or Threatened

A Joint Petition should represent voluntary agreement. Threats, coercion or serious pressure should not be treated as an ordinary agreed Joint Petition.

## This Does Not Mean You Cannot Get Divorced

It means that LegalDIY's current Joint Petition pathway may not fit your circumstances. Other legal procedures may be available.

Where you cannot proceed together or there is a substantial dispute, consider obtaining independent advice from a qualified legal professional.
